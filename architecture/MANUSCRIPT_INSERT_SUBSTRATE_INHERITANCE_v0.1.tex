\paragraph{Validated-substrate inheritance and its boundary.}
A converged clean-surface calculation is treated here as reusable numerical infrastructure, not as a transferable chemical result.  Once a substrate representation has passed its frozen thickness, vacuum, reciprocal-space, force, and independent-reproduction gates, a later calculation on the same physical surface may inherit that clean-substrate qualification when the substrate-defining computational model is materially unchanged.  Thus a compatible new adsorbate on the same qualified Cu(111) representation need not repeat the full clean-slab convergence campaign solely because the adsorbate identity changes.  What is inherited is the numerical certificate that the finite slab adequately represents the specified clean substrate under the pinned electronic-structure and electrostatic model; entry-specific adsorption, lateral-cell, reaction-path, saddle, vibrational, friction, and rate quantities remain separately qualified.

The transfer is intentionally fail-closed.  The clean-surface certificate does not automatically extend to another material or facet, a reconstructed, defective, alloyed, oxidized, strongly strained, charged, field-biased, explicitly solvated, or otherwise materially different surface state, or a calculation that changes the exchange-correlation functional, pseudopotential family, electrostatic boundary treatment, or another substrate-defining part of the Hamiltonian.  Nor does clean-slab convergence establish lateral adsorbate-image convergence, adsorption-site ordering, barrier convergence, a reaction-coordinate Hessian, dissipation, or ChemSA eligibility.  These exclusions follow from what a slab-convergence test actually proves: insensitivity of the clean-surface result to the numerical boundaries that were varied for one specified substrate model, not invariance under a different physical state or model.  If a downstream calculation changes a substrate-defining assumption or reveals a perturbation outside that validated scope, only the affected numerical dimension is prospectively requalified; unaffected, hash-verified substrate evidence remains reusable.  For CO/Cu(111), this inheritance statement remains conditional until the current frozen clean-surface convergence gate returns PASS.
